Middle Floor

The Barthog store — two rooms that held the household's provisions through the year.

Traditional Storage

The Barthog.

The middle floor, known as the Barthog, with its two rooms, served as a storeroom. Here, one can experience the traditional Bhutanese methods of storage — timber bins, hanging racks and dry, wind-sheltered rooms built to keep a household's provisions through the year.

The Barthog's two rooms hold the household's wine and spirit containers, butter and cheese vessels, and a sacred grain vault of red rice, stored in bham and luep and measured by the old phita, dre and standard weighing scales.

What's Here

Two rooms, a year's provisions.

  • Wine and spirit containers for the household's Ara and other brews
  • Butter and cheese vessels, kept cool in the wind-sheltered rooms
  • A sacred grain vault of red rice, stored in bham and luep containers
  • The old phita, dre and standard weighing scales once used to measure it out

Kept Dry, Kept Safe

A storeroom by design.

Sandwiched between the ground floor's stable and the top floor's living quarters, the Barthog's timber bins and hanging racks kept a household's wine, dairy and grain dry and out of reach of animals below — a working larder at the centre of the house.
