Every membership, internship and donation keeps a tradition alive — here's how you can be part of it.

Why It Matters

A living heritage, kept alive by its community.

Folk Heritage is more than a collection behind glass — it's a working craft village, a restored 18th-century manor, and a place where traditional skills are still taught, practised and passed on.

None of that continues on its own. Members, interns, volunteers, donors and partners each play a part in conserving the collection, supporting local artisans, and keeping the manor open to the next generation of visitors.

Ways to Get Involved

Membership, Internships & Volunteering

Three ways to spend time with the collection, the artisans and the grounds — from a single afternoon to a full season.

Full membership details are still being developed. Register your interest via the contact page and we'll let you know as soon as it launches.

A one-month internship with Folk Heritage includes a one-year museum membership, a completion certificate, three complimentary entries, free program participation, priority seasonal access, one complimentary learning session, and a 20% discount at the shop and Craft Village.

Volunteers who complete 72 hours of service receive a certificate of service, two complimentary entries, program and workshop access, free selected program participation, and priority workshop registration.

Donation

Donate a Collection

Supporters can fund the conservation of a specific object in the collection. In return, donors receive recognition through exhibition display, an appreciation certificate, and annual updates on how their contribution has been used.

Donation categories and their conservation costs will be listed here soon — contact us for current options.

Partnership

Collaboration & Partnership

University Partnerships

Heritage research, museum studies, anthropology, history and architecture; digital documentation; and student internships on defined projects.

Professional Services

Photographers, videographers, IT professionals, researchers, content developers, translators, marketing professionals and conservation specialists.

Public Heritage Documentation

Community contributions of traditional recipes, songs, tool knowledge, occupational histories, historical photographs and household practices.
