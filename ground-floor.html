Room 02 of 04 · Inside the Manor

Ground Floor

Storehouse and stable — three rooms that once pulsed with the rhythms of rural life.

See Medieval Bhutan

Built for the animals first.

Stroll through the three-room ground floor, a bustling storehouse and shelter for domestic animals in the past. Its rammed-earth walls and low, stone-flagged rooms once kept grain, tools and livestock safe through Thimphu's winters.

Three rooms pulse with the rhythms of rural life: a store of saddles, ropes and tack for the beasts of burden, a room for pregnant and milking cows, and Buchung-go ray — a dedicated space for calves.

What's Here

Three rooms, one working floor.

  • A tack store of saddles, ropes and other gear for the household's beasts of burden
  • A room set aside for pregnant and milking cows
  • Buchung-go ray, a dedicated space for calves, kept apart from the herd

Rammed Earth & Stone

A floor built to endure.

The ground floor's thick rammed-earth walls and stone-flagged rooms were built to withstand the weight of stored grain and the wear of livestock — the same construction that has kept the house standing on its original foundations for more than two centuries.
