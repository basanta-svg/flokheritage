Inside the Manor

Entrance

First impressions, the stone-paved courtyard and timber facade that set the tone for everything inside.

Courtyard – gaegho

In most traditional Bhutanese houses, one would find an enclosed courtyard. All the domestic animals, such as cows, horses, and chickens, are kept in the courtyard at night to protect them from wild animals. Many other activities, such as roasting rice (zaw), brewing local drinks (Ara), grinding and dehusking grams, and weaving cloth, were also done in the courtyard.

Also, farming equipment such as a plough, a yoke, an ox-drawn wooden leveller, thrashers (korbji), and rolled bamboo mats for drying grains and vegetables is kept in the courtyard. Here, visitors should not miss discovering two kinds of ploughshare, one made of wood and the other made of iron. The iron ploughshare is a reminder of the advent of iron mining and smelting, which started in the 14th century, after the great saint Thangthong Gyelpo (1361 AD–1475 AD), the master iron chain bridge builder, introduced the process of iron mining and smelting to Bhutan.
