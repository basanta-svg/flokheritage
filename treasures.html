Treasures

The Folk Heritage Museum offers a journey back in time, immersing visitors in the medieval era. The centuries-old priceless artifacts within highlight various significant aspects of Bhutanese life in the past.

The Heritage

A Voyage Into Bhutan's Past

Embark on an enchanting journey into Bhutan's past, a voyage akin to turning the pages of a storied history book. Yet here, the narrative unfurls through the visual splendor of antique pots and pans, ancient weapons, venerable weighing scales, and traditional garments.

The air is rich with the aroma of a medieval kitchen, and the sanctity of spaces where ancestors invoked deities enhances the immersive experience. No sojourn through Bhutanese culture is complete without a visit to the Heritage.

Traditional Weighing Scales

Sang, Goe & Tiru Sangchung

Measurement

Three standard weighing scales once used across Bhutanese households: the Sang (roughly 333.32 grams, or a third of a kilogram), the Goe (equivalent to six Sang), and the Tiru Sangchung — a silversmith's scale calibrated against a Victorian coin weight of roughly 11.66 grams per mark.

Cham Dance Costume

Silk brocade & appliqué, 20th century

Textile

A hand-embroidered cham costume in yellow and red brocade, worn by monastic dancers during masked festival performances. The skirt's floral appliqué motifs are typical of workshops in central Bhutan, where costumes are made and re-made for each generation of dancers rather than kept as static museum pieces.

Carved Rabsel Window

Cypress, hand-carved

Woodwork

A traditional rabsel bay window, its lattice carved from a single cypress panel and left unpainted to show the tool marks of the carpenter. Windows of this type project from the upper storey of a house and were reserved for the family shrine room or the head of household's quarters.

Hilltop Chorten Model

Lime render over stone, votive scale

Ritual Object

A votive chorten built at reduced scale, its whitewashed lime render and gilded finial modelled on the reliquary stupas that mark ridgelines across the valley. Small chortens like this are commissioned to accumulate merit on behalf of a household, and are sited deliberately — this one facing the same ridge as the temple it was modelled on.

Monastic Robe

Wool, natural dye

Ritual Object

A maroon monastic robe, dyed in small batches using madder root and walnut husk. The deep, slightly uneven colour is characteristic of natural dye lots — no two robes from the same batch are ever identical, which is part of why they are valued over synthetic alternatives.

Guardian Mask

Carved wood, gesso & pigment

Ritual Object

A carved wooden guardian mask, coated in gesso and painted in mineral pigment, worn during the black-hat cham to represent protective deities. Masks of this scale are carved from a single block of willow or cypress, hollowed from the reverse to reduce weight for the dancer.

Ceremonial Kabney

Raw silk, handwoven

Textile

A handwoven silk scarf, worn draped over the shoulder as a mark of rank and occasion. The particular weave and undyed cream tone shown here identifies the wearer's status within a formal gathering — a system of colour and cloth that predates any written record of it.
